SLIDE 2 - THE PROBLEM

Title:
Blind customers are still banking through interfaces built for sight

Body:
- Mainstream banking apps assume visual verification
- Screen readers read what exists, but they do not redesign the flow
- Blind users still struggle to confirm amounts, scan transactions, and
  trust payment steps independently

Closer line:
Accessibility retrofits do not create confidence. They create
workarounds.

SLIDE 3 - THE USER

Title:
Who we built this for

Body:
Our user is a blind customer who wants to bank independently, safely,
and without relying on another person to read the screen or verify a
payment.

Key needs:
- hear balance and account state clearly
- understand recent activity quickly
- send money safely
- avoid scams and pressure tactics
- hear bank messages without outside help

SLIDE 4 - THE SOLUTION

Title:
EchoBank is a voice-first banking experience

Body:
EchoBank is designed around hearing, speech, touch, and confidence.
Instead of adapting a visual app after the fact, we design the banking
journey so a blind customer can use it directly.

What it does:
- spoken balance and account summary
- audio-guided activity review
- safer payment flow
- accessible message reading
- preference controls for speech, haptics, and sound cues

SLIDE 5 - WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

Title:
This is not just accessible. It is safer.

Body:
Most accessibility tools help a blind customer use an existing system.
EchoBank changes the system itself.

Core differentiators:
- audio-first design
- earcons for transaction categories
- scam-risk checks before payment
- confirmation designed for blind trust, not blind clicking

SLIDE 8 - WOW FACTOR

Title:
Safe Circle Verify

Body:
If a payment looks risky, EchoBank can say:
"This does not look safe. Would you like to ask someone you trust?"

What happens next:

- it explains the amount, payee, and risk signals
- the user can escalate to a trusted contact before money moves

Why it matters:

- reduces pressure-based fraud
- creates a memorable product edge
