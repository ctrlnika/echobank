1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS

EchoBank is a prototype banking app designed for people who are fully
blind or have very severe visual impairment. The main idea is that
banking should not depend on reading a screen. Instead, the app should
work through speech, sound, simple touch interactions, and very clear
accessible layouts.

This is not just a normal banking app with accessibility added later.
The product idea is "voice first". That means the spoken experience is
meant to be the primary experience, and the visual screen supports it.

The app is built around a challenge brief about helping a blind banking
customer use digital banking independently and safely.

2. THE PROBLEM IT IS TRYING TO SOLVE

Standard banking apps usually assume the user can:
- read balances on screen
- visually scan transactions
- inspect account details before paying someone
- trust a popup confirmation they cannot independently verify
- read letters and statements visually

For a blind customer, that creates dependence on other people and
increases the risk of mistakes and scams.

EchoBank tries to solve that by making the key flows work through:
- spoken summaries
- voice interactions
- audio cues
- haptic confirmation
- high-contrast screens as a backup, not the only interface

The goal is independent, safe banking.

3. THE CORE IDEA IN ONE SENTENCE

EchoBank is a voice-first accessible banking app for blind users that
helps them check balances, review activity, send money safely, and hear
their bank messages without relying on sight.

5. THE PRODUCT STORY IN SIMPLE LANGUAGE

Imagine a blind customer opening a banking app. Instead of being faced
with a screen they must explore visually, the app can immediately say:

"You have 1,284 pounds and 50 pence available. Your latest transaction
was 32 pounds and 10 pence at Tesco. You have 2 unread letters."

If they want to send money, the app should help them do it safely by:
- identifying who they are paying
- speaking the amount back clearly
- checking for scam signals
- requiring a deliberate confirmation action

If they receive a letter or statement, they should be able to hear it
read aloud rather than needing sighted assistance.

That is the main value proposition: accessible, independent, safer
banking.

9. HOW PAYMENTS WORK

The payment flow is one of the most important parts of the app.

From the code, the flow appears to be:

1. user chooses a saved payee or types a new one
2. user enters an amount
3. the app can save new payee details
4. the app runs a scam check before sending
5. the app speaks back the payment details
6. the user confirms
7. the payment server function executes the transfer

The code also includes a hold-to-confirm component. That matters because
the project is trying to avoid unsafe blind confirmation patterns where
the user just taps an "OK" button they cannot verify independently.

10. HOW SCAM CHECKING WORKS

There is a deterministic scam/risk engine in the server code.

It looks for things such as:
- new payees
- untrusted payees
- unusually large payments
- out-of-hours activity
- urgency or secrecy language in the request context
- impersonation-style language

This is a strong part of the concept, because accessible banking is not
just about readability. It is also about safety and confidence.
